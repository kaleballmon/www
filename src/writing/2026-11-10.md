It\'s a heavy task for me to drop, or avoid holding in the first place, struggles that are not mine but try to fall into my hands.
This mostly comes in the form of the work I do to make living: internal conflicts that are tangential to my job, client drama, etc.
It also occasionally comes in the form of familial turmoil, friend beef, and neighborhood drama; none of which pertains to me but tries to find crevices into my life sphere.
I\'m glad to have a strongle muscle of dealing with this, reinforced by _peace_ being amongst the top desires for my life.
This level of awareness I have towards this particular social phenomenon shows up in what I choose to bottle up and bear myself as well:
not asking for help when I need it, processing emotions alone, trying to protect people when it\'s not my duty. I understand that this isn\'t
entirely healthy and I work to get better at this balance, but as I live more I stand by my \"side of the fence\" more firmly.

The default modus operandi the \(Western\) world assumes is to externalize and to outsource. Labor, thinking, waste.
Dealing with something is often approached as taking something that is here and moving it somewhere else,
rather than handling the thing at the source. And in a physical sense, we are becoming more concious of this as climate concerns grow,
labor is put into question by the laborers, and we are actively seeing the consolidation of thinking to a handful of
\"artificial intelligence\" companies. However, to this day, the mainstream still does not fully consider the non-physical, emotional, spiritual, and moral, externalization that happens within every action
that casts a vote, loud or silently, to continue life as normal in a patriarchal and capitalist society.

And I wonder what the day will look like  
when we simply cannot hold more.
