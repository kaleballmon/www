title: "Right tool for the job"
date: 2026-11-24
date_color: "#A6A9AA"
---

The most engineer thing about me is that I'm obsessed with tools. I think the
default engineer love is this abstract idea of "building" but I have a
complicated relationship with notion of "building" because I think people build
too much sometimes which is a different conversation.

Back to tools though, I get unreasonably excited in the hardware store and when
I stumble across some new open source software online. I especially love tools
that are not only for creating a new thing, but are also for the _preserverance_
of a thing.

On the software tip, one thing that I've started to break for myself is my
skepticism of
[graphical user interfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_user_interface)
or GUIs as they're called amongst the nerds. Recently, a coworker of mine showed
me a new tool which was essentially a GUI around a tool I already use but in my
[terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_emulator). As I've mentioned,
I was naturally skeptical. Almost unreasonably skeptical. What would buttons and
fancy design over something I already used and knew how to use well going to
help me. I'm the type of person that doesn't knock something until I try it
though so I tried it. It was so fucking good.

As I've gotten further in my engineering career, and also just meeting engineers
in other fields, I've started to realize that the best engineers are always
willing to use the right tool for the right job, even if it usurps their ol'
reliable tool, or even goes against their personal preferences.

So I sat and asked myself what does a "good tool" mean for me. What I've found
in myself is a large disdain for hype and chasing the next shiny thing. Some
call this cynicism or being jaded. I do think this is has historically impared
be from doing my best work as an engineer or as an artist, however. Sometimes
you just gotta ask yourself what's the right tool for the job.
