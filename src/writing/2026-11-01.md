I\'m an engineer by trade. I descend from the lineage of open source, continuous collaboration, and energy/information exchange. Professionally, working in the open has always been my core tenet so my reticense around doing the same with other practices in my life has been an internal conflict raging for years at this point. I don\'t care to unpack why this is the case for me or how I\'ve arived at my current practice where everything is shared, but I do want to talk about Playboi Carti and Roy Ayers.

I\'m a child of mid-2010\'s cracked versions of FL Studio, Reddit drum kits, and Guitar Center MIDI controllers. At the time I completely missed the point of most of it. I was insanely precious about everything. Spent days on beats, only sharing them to rapper friends if they happened to be looking over my shoulder and seeing a .wav file and ask about it. Beats and songs would stay on SoundCloud for 36 hours before I psyched myself into taking them down. I would tell people \"I play around with music stuff\" knowing damn well I was logging two full-time job\'s worth of hours making and tweaking and trying to perfect shit.

I\'m also a child of snippet and leak culture, but I fully understood that. The concept of perfect moments over perfect pieces made sense to me, and I could see what that did for artists in terms hype and for testing the waters. I was also intimately familiar with the act of overthinking, so I was never disappointed when the leak never fully dropped or the released version never really hit the same. Sometimes I was glad it stayed a leak. This is why I loved Playboi Carti. He often moved as if the leak was the point. It was open source in motion. You came to understand him and his music more intimately because you saw it from the front, back, sides, distored, saturated, stretched, compressed, etc. When something exists in that way, processes of synthesis, deconstruction, and reconstruction are able to happen that multiply the lives it has. When I first heard the 24 second snippet for _Foreign_, me and my friend Devin sat in the library and ran it back I don\'t know how many times. I went home the same night and fired up FL Studio and looped it for 2 minutes and put it on SoundCloud. I even fucked with the EQ a bit. When _Die Lit_ dropped with the full version of _Foreign_ on it I still ran my version from time to time for a different feeling. I had crafted my own lens and I loved it.

As I developed into a jazz, soul, and R&B head, I feel the same energy in the demo. Demos can inform and enhance what you already know. Demos can make you question what you think you already know. Demos can build bridges. I want to give praise to [Roy Ayers\' _Virgin Ubiquity_ unreleased recordings series](https://royayers.bandcamp.com/album/virgin-ubiquity-ii-unreleased-recordings-1976-1981). I get really excited about this but also hate being long-winded so I\'m going to briefly mention _Sunshine (Demo)_ and the freestyle Roy breaks out towards the end of the song:

> In the sunshine, it\'s so warm and tender.
>
> Makes your body so wet you must surrender.
>
> Sunshine,
>
> I got to make you mine.
>
> Say you will stay,
>
> and never go away.

> Hey, hey.
>
> What you say?
>
> Where you going?
>
> How you doing?
>
> How you feeling?
>
> Every day of your life
>
> live and love the sunshine.

This isn\'t on the album version, but the demo freestyle shows the space and possibility in the structure of the song, adding more lenses and more lives.

I don\'t know how significant that space is to me without me knowing the demo. _Foreign_ is probably not one of my favorite Playboi Carti songs without the snippet. I\'m praying my drafts and explorations unlock something for me, or for somebody else.
