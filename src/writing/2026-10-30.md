I remember Brother Gerald vividly.

In my eyes, he was a professional athlete. I think he believed he could run or jump to Heaven, or that getting there required some of both. He was a star. A real star. You know that feeling when you see a star and you get that jolt but you try to play it cool type star. You get clout being the pastor\'s grandchild, but sharing blood with the popular deacon is cool in a way that extends farther into the world, past where Jesus seemingly wants to go. The type of cool that when he put his hand on my shoulder and said my name, before giving me praise or instruction or telling a joke or giving me a task passed down the chain of communication from my grandparents, I felt: \"Yea, y\'all can\'t fuck with me\". I felt like a black-man-in-training learning from _the_ black man.

And he was in the prayer circle when I first caught the Holy Ghost. I remember his prayers feeling like spells for both the spiritual and the physical \"Yes, save his soul Lord, but also grow him into a prosporous adult and make him safe and make him fulfilled and make him curious\".

Him and my PawPaw went together. Same year, 5 months apart. I was 13. He was 47. I don\'t know why it didn\'t feel like a tragedy then. Maybe because I was petrified about [the world ending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_phenomenon). Maybe I was still wrapped up in my own, more personal, grief. I think I didn\'t really know how old he was and him going felt _natural_.

I\'m working to have the same amount of magic he had when summoning a fleet of protection around me. I wish I was able to reciprocate it then. Black folk are still so focused on the future.
